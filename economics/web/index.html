Model limitations & sensitivity

    Priority and fuel assumptions

    Five vehicle minimum-green timings (8, 15, 30, 45 and 60 seconds) are tested in each of four independent demand periods: 625 schedules plus baseline fallback. Both priorities prohibit extra combined road-user waiting. CO2 priority maximizes net savings after device electricity within that constraint; Time priority minimizes pedestrian and occupancy-weighted vehicle waiting subject to nonnegative net CO2 savings. If both targets cannot be met, the least-emitting time-safe option is reported with an unmet-target warning, not fabricated savings. Both modes can legitimately select the same schedule. Yellow, all-red and pedestrian clearance settings remain unchanged. Period transitions are not simulated, and the daily profile is provisional. Selection uses the same simulated samples as appraisal, not independent validation or citywide routing. Individual pedestrian waiting can still increase even when aggregate road-user waiting falls. No observed district-specific ranking is available.

    The default compact-car no-load idle reference is 0.62 litres per engine-on hour (10.3 mL/minute), not litres per kilometre. It is not a measured Turin fleet average. Registered petrol/diesel share and assumed engine-on share reduce modelled consumption further. Annual fuel totals cover all equipped crossings; repeat visits are crossing events, not unique cars. Driving, acceleration and restart fuel are not included.

    Data provenance

    Calculation boundary

    Paired Poisson arrivals feed the same òcio signal controller used by the simulator. Each demand period has a 5-minute warm-up and a 30-minute measurement, repeated with four seeds. Two road lanes discharge at 1,800 vehicles/hour/lane. Pedestrians cross at 0.8–1.6 m/s; no detector frames, route assignment or intersection coordination are simulated.

    Demand follows a provisional 24-hour profile: night 6h at 8% of peak, off-peak 10h at 45%, shoulder 4h at 70%, peak 4h at 100%. The profile and daily pedestrian rates are not measured citywide. Both demand-actuated policies retain the same button fallback; vision can register a request sooner. During assumed downtime the baseline controller remains active.

    Annual time value = (pedestrian hours + vehicle hours × occupancy) × time value. Fuel = stationary vehicle hours × idle litres/hour × modelled petrol/diesel share × engine-running share. Net operational CO2 subtracts device electricity emissions. Annual social value adds time, fuel and carbon values, then subtracts routine maintenance, electricity and average major replacement cost. NPV discounts actual yearly flows and includes installed cost, shared setup and replacement. The default replacement budget is 10% of units cumulatively across ten years, spread as 1% per year, at the installed prototype price.

    GDP contribution is a separate conditional gross-value-added proxy from work and net-new, non-overlapping spending. Its conversion inputs are unmeasured assumptions; it is not causal GDP growth and is excluded from NPV to avoid counting time twice. Safety, pollution other than CO2, induced demand, embodied carbon, new signals and civil works are outside this model. Actual procurement and deployment require surveyed crossings and certified controller integration.
